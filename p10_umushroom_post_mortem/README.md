# P10 — UMushroom UK Investment Competition 2026: post-mortem

[Portfolio home](../README.md) · [P9, the pre-trade risk model](../p9_umushroom_event_risk/)

P9 set out the risk of a two-person £100k paper portfolio (team "The V² Fund") before the competition window opened. This project scores that portfolio after the window closed. It rebuilds the book from the platform's transaction ledger, explains the loss by source, measures the loss against P9's frozen forecast, rechecks the six report-day calls, and tests the rules the team wrote after the loss. Everything is valued at the 20 Nov 2026 close, the last session of Stage 1.

Its outputs supplied the numbers in the team's final strategy Description on the platform. The submitted text is kept in [`data/input/description_final_submitted.txt`](data/input/description_final_submitted.txt). Test T41 checks every numeric token in it against the outputs in `out/` and a list of fixed facts (dates and rule constants), with one documented exception, the exchange rate 1.335 (see Limitations); the range 81–97% is checked against the attribution range directly. Test T43b ties its key sentences to the fields they quote.

**Protocol.** P9's model, data cutoff (6 Nov) and call ledger were fixed before the window and are read here without change; this folder only reads P9's output files. Estimation in this project uses data up to the 6 Nov cutoff unless a component says otherwise, and `src/data.py` asserts it. The thesis rules for the scorecard were written after the loss. The first estimates snapshot (19 Nov) informed the 2% estimates threshold: Home Depot's decline was already visible in it. Both estimates snapshots existed when the rules were hashed at 21:00:26 UTC on 21 Nov (the second was downloaded at 20:58:35 UTC), and the final flags were recorded after the hash. The hashed file is kept byte for byte as `data/input/thesis_rules.original.json`, with its digest in `thesis_rules.lock`. The code reads a publication copy, `thesis_rules.json`, which makes three wording edits listed in `src/scorecard.py` and has its own digest in `thesis_rules.published.lock`; test T25 checks both digests and the edits. The Description says the rules came after the loss.

## Method

1. **Ledger (`ledger.py`).** Daily sterling NAV from `data/input/transactions.csv`, reconciled to the platform snapshots. Fill prices imply the FX rate the platform used.
2. **Attribution (`attribution.py`).** Each holding's daily sterling P&L split exactly into market, orthogonalised sector, FX, FX cross term, scheduled report-day abnormal return, unscheduled-event abnormal return, residual, crypto, execution (fill to close) and the Spyre satellite. Betas come from 1 May – 6 Nov, the P9 window. The 36 variants change the beta window, the event window, ETF against ex-self peer baskets, and alpha in or out; buckets sum to the P&L in every variant (T3).
3. **Forecast check (`power.py`, `plan2_core.py`, `p9_replica.py`).** z-scores and VaR flags against P9's published figures; a Yahoo-data replication of P9 (4.05% two-week s.d., test T10 accepts 3.6–4.2%); rolling windows, block bootstrap and a closed-form power table showing how little ten sessions can reveal.
4. **Counterfactual (`counterfactual.py`).** P9's planned, halfway and equal-risk books, formed at the actual 7 Nov and 9 Nov fill prices and held unchanged.
5. **Strategic restatement (`strategic.py`, `decide.py`).** Weekly sterling returns, Ledoit–Wolf covariance, sleeve risk parity with a 15% cap, and a decision table from scorecard status, current weight, target weight and the deployment triggers.
6. **Scorecard (`scorecard.py`).** Four thesis rules (guidance cut, falling estimates, regulatory or accounting event, unplanned chief executive or finance chief exit), written after the loss with the first estimates snapshot in view, and hashed on 21 Nov before the final flags were recorded; status is a pure function of `data/input/thesis_evidence.csv`.
7. **Observed-decline stress (`stress.py`).** Applies each holding's realised 6–20 Nov sterling move to the held, trimmed and full-budget books, once with every holding shocked and once with only the AI and crypto names.
8. **Base rates (`drawdown.py`, `event_study.py`, `horizon.py`, `fundamentals.py`).** How often a fall of this size happened before and what followed, with Bitcoin sampled on US sessions like the book. Beat-and-fell events for the four US names since 2002. Each report is a beat, miss or tie by the sign of Yahoo's reported surprise; the comparison of EPS rounded to cents is used only where the surprise is missing, because rounding had turned 65 beats into ties. Only events whose 60-session outcome window ends by the 6 Nov cutoff are kept: 382 reports, after removing four rows with neither a surprise nor a reported EPS, one repeated row, two reports listed twice with opposite surprises (Walmart, August 2002; NVIDIA, November 2006) and five late reports, each counted in `results.json`. Of the 326 beats, 148 had a negative abnormal return on the reaction day; over the next 60 sessions those 148 averaged −0.81% (95% interval −2.4% to 0.73%), so the history gives no evidence either way. The intervals resample whole quarters to match the clustered test. Ten-session R² on each equity's own trading sessions, and the share of daily variance on report days, with report days taken from every dated release (with or without reported EPS) on the full trading calendar; price against trailing earnings built from four consecutive fiscal quarters, at exact quarter distances; forward EPS revisions against price.

## Running it

```
./run_all.sh              # rebuild out/ from the saved snapshot in data/raw/, then run the tests
./run_all.sh --download   # take a fresh Yahoo snapshot first
```

`run_all.sh` creates `.venv` from `requirements.txt` (Python 3.9 was used) on first run. It needs the sibling folder `../p9_umushroom_event_risk/`, whose `out/` files it reads. Tests alone: `.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q tests/`.

**Data.** Prices, dividends and analyst estimates come from Yahoo Finance through `yfinance`. As with P9, they are not redistributed: `data/raw/` is ignored by git, so on a fresh clone `run_all.sh` downloads a new snapshot. The published `out/` was built from the snapshot taken on 21 Nov 2026 at 20:59 UTC (estimates at 20:58 UTC, plus three earlier estimate snapshots from 19 Nov). From that snapshot the pipeline reproduces every file in `out/` byte for byte; a new download will be close but not identical, because Yahoo revises adjusted closes and the estimates are taken on the day you run it.

**Tests.** `tests/test_all.py` holds 57 tests: ledger identities, leakage, synthetic recovery for the attribution and base-rate code, seed stability, the decision and scorecard logic, the stress test, the 25 Nov mark to market, the ledger-hash correction, strict JSON, date bounds on the platform reading, the event-study classification, the G2 claim gate, and checks on the submitted Description. On the saved snapshot 48 pass and 9 skip. The nine skipped cases (T13, T30, T31 and three cases each of T33 and T35) check the team's intermediate Description drafts, which are not included here; T41 and T30b apply the same checks to the submitted text. T41 is a numeric-token check: every number in the Description must match a value in `out/` or a listed fixed fact (dates, rule constants), with one documented exception, 1.335, and the range 81–97% checked against the attribution range; it does not check which claim a number supports. T43b ties the key sentences, including the sleeve weights and the two offsets, to their specific fields, and fails if the 16% and 11% offsets or two sleeves are swapped. Tests that pin figures from the saved snapshot skip on a fresh download rather than fail; T43 and T45 still run their identities there and check the archived amounts only on the archived snapshot. T14 originally checked that a separate read-only copy of this repository was untouched; it now checks that P9's `model.py` and `out/` are unchanged since commit 53af9f5, the version these results used.

## Limitations

- **Ten sessions.** One two-week outcome cannot separate a mis-sized forecast from a bad draw; a z of −2.2 on one draw is weak evidence about calibration. Six calls cannot show skill either way. Not reported: a Sharpe over 10 sessions has a standard error of about 5.4 annualised.
- **Yahoo data.** Closes, FX and estimates are from Yahoo through `yfinance`, not the platform's own price feed. The rebuilt NAV is £26.90 below the platform's, and the platform's FX on fills differs from Yahoo's closes (the 7 Nov crypto buys imply 1.3357 dollars per pound against a Yahoo close of 1.3456). The 19 Nov platform reading was taken before the US close, so its rebuild is a mixed-time reconstruction: platform quotes where the reading gives them, otherwise that evening's Yahoo closes and exchange rate. It is used for the unit counts, not for any result.
- **Attribution depends on the specification.** The systematic share runs from 80.5% to 97.2% across 36 reasonable variants, so it is quoted as a range. The mechanical unscheduled-event rule missed NVIDIA's chief executive exit on 12 Nov (abnormal return −3.6%, below the 2.5 s.d. flag) and flagged the CPI day instead; both readings are in `results.json`.
- **The observed-decline stress is in sample.** Its shocks are the same window's realised moves, so it describes what that fall would do to other books; it is not an independent forecast.
- **The rules came after the loss.** The thesis rules, the risk-parity targets and the deployment audit were written on 21 Nov, after the fall. The first estimates snapshot informed the 2% threshold, and both snapshots existed before the hash. The hash shows the rules were fixed before the final flags were recorded; it does not show they came before the evidence, and it does nothing to limit fitting to the loss itself. The rule text calls the second snapshot "S2 = 22 Nov"; the one used was downloaded on 21 Nov at 20:58:35 UTC.
- **Hindsight in the base rates.** The event study covers four tickers chosen because the team held them; the drawdown base rates use the team's own weights.
- **The ledger hash in the frozen book.** `out/book_frozen_20261120.json` records the hash of a ledger that dated the Spyre buy 20 Nov; the published ledger dates it 19 Nov (the platform shows it "Added On 19 Nov 2026" and the fill equals the 19 Nov close). Final units, cash, closing NAV and cumulative P&L are the same under both dates. The daily path is not: with the buy on 19 Nov, that day's NAV is £6.78 higher and the 20 Nov daily P&L is −£3,721.49 rather than −£3,714.71. The correction is recorded in `out/book_frozen_20261120.correction.json` and test T43 checks it. The same record notes that the book's platform NAV field (£91,466.50) comes from the platform reading documented at about 21:50 UTC, after the book's 21:09:11 UTC stamp, so that field is a later annotation.
- **Fill wording in the submitted text.** The Description credits the 16% execution offset to 9 Nov. It is the execution bucket's net of 7, 9 and 13 Nov fills, of which 9 Nov (+£1,424.67, mainly Applied Materials) is almost all, as a share of the rebuilt loss of £8,560.40. The bucket leaves out the Ethereum and Spyre fills, booked on their own lines; with them, fills total +£1,415.56 (16.5%).
- **Currency offset in the submitted text.** The Description says sterling's fall offset 11% of the loss. That figure is the FX bucket of the four core US equities: £944.27 of the rebuilt £8,560.40 loss. Bitcoin, Ethereum and Spyre are also quoted in dollars; their currency effect, computed the same way (£350.27), sits inside their own buckets. Across every dollar-quoted holding, sterling's fall offset £1,294.54, or 15.1% (`results.json` `B.fx_scope`). The text was locked when submitted, so the scope is recorded here.
- **Five-year horizon in the submitted text.** The Description calls five years the primary horizon. The dated 7 Nov call ledger (P9's `ledger.csv`) instead set each position to hold to the 20 Nov close unless guidance was cut. The five-year wording is an account written after the loss, not the plan recorded before it.
- **The G2 intervals are too narrow.** In test T18's synthetic null (1,000 panels with no true difference), the block-bootstrap 95% interval for the 20-quarter minus 1-quarter difference excludes zero in 16.8% of panels rather than 5%, and the 99% interval in 7.7% rather than 1% (17.2% and 6.4% on a fresh set of panels), even with each company's means recomputed in every draw as the point estimate does: about 100 quarters with 20-quarter overlaps leave too few blocks. A rise is claimed only when the 99% lower bound is above zero and at least three of the four companies rise. That bar gave 28 false claims in 1,000 panels, on the original panels and on a fresh set (the 95% bar: 72 and 71). The earlier estimator, which kept each company's means fixed across draws, gave 73 with the 99% bar and 115 with the 95% bar; `out/t18.json` keeps all of these counts. The bar is not met here (only Applied Materials and Home Depot rise), every G2 interval is descriptive only, and no G2 figure is used in the Description.
- **A known discrepancy in the submitted text.** The Description says sterling fell "from 1.335 to 1.309" dollars since 7 Nov. The platform's implied 7 Nov rate is 1.3357, which rounds to 1.336. The text was locked when submitted, so the error is recorded rather than corrected; test T41 pins it.
- **Working notes not included.** Code comments and input files refer to planning documents (PLAN, PLAN2, PLAN3, CONTEXT) and to Description drafts kept outside this repository.
- **Working labels removed after the fact.** Before publication, internal working labels in a few input files, code strings and `out/book_frozen_20261120.json` were reworded (for example "EXIT unless the team documents a case"); no number changed, and the frozen book's SHA-256 was re-pinned on 25 Nov. The thesis rules are the exception: the hashed original is kept unchanged beside the reworded publication copy (see Protocol). Company officers are named by role only, and source links whose addresses contained a person's name were replaced by the outlet, date and headline.
- **Single source for some inputs.** Some news inputs were secondary reports or could not be fetched; `data/input/thesis_evidence.csv` and `data/input/macro_events.csv` mark each source's class, including "not sourced" and "UNVERIFIED".
